📋 Step-by-Step Cutting Plan
1

Watch the primary video end-to-end first

Watch The One Minute Barber video above without touching your hair. Then rewatch the scissor-over-comb sides segment and the fringe chapter immediately before you start.

2

Watch one short dry/curl-by-curl clip

Before cutting, watch the Sam Villa curl-cutting reference (Curl-by-Curl tab above) so you understand cutting in natural curl formation and snipping at the point a curl loses its pattern.

3

Prep: style hair as normal, detangle, clip the top away

Cut dry if possible — it's lower-risk for a beginner because you see exactly what you're removing. (Experts disagree: Sam Villa prefers wet for control. See Tips below.)

4

Scissor-over-comb on the sides — conservative first pass

Start at the back/sides. Use scissor-over-comb (from primary video mid-section) to remove a small amount of bulk around the ears. Stop and reassess before going further.

5

Refine ends with point-cutting where needed

Use the Joewell/Sam Villa point-cut images as your hand-position reference — scissors vertical, tiny wedges, partial closure. Go lightest on 2c clumps or keep those ends blunter.

6

Fringe / middle-part front last

Cut the fringe longer than your target — curl spring will lift it. Follow the fringe chapter (~11:00) of the primary video.

💡 Tips, Caveats & Benchmarks

Dry (recommended for beginners): Cut hair in its natural curl formation so you see true shrinkage and shape. You know exactly what you're removing. Lower-risk for a conservative first-timer cleanup.

Wet (Sam Villa's view): "Cut the hair wet. You'll have greater control of the curl and of your sectioning with wet hair, because you'll be reading the natural silhouette. Wet hair will not expand and frizz." — American Salon

Both are valid professional approaches. For a conservative home trim, dry is the safer starting point.

Curls spring up when dry. If you cut to the length you want while wet, the hair will shrink up shorter when dry. Always leave extra length — you can always remove more, you can't put it back.

This applies especially to the fringe/middle-part front. Cut it visibly longer than your target; let it dry and re-assess.

Sam Villa: "If you point cut naturally curly hair, it will take the weight away from ends, causing the ends of the hair to expand and look frizzy."

On your 2b (wavier) sections: you can point-cut fairly freely.

On your 2c (tighter) clumps: keep the ends blunter. Use the "Weave & Blunt" approach — weave into the hair section, then close your shear where you want to take weight away. This removes bulk while preserving the end weight that holds the curl together.

Signal to stop point-cutting: if ends look stringy or frizzy after notching, you've removed too much end weight — stop immediately on those sections.

Scissor-over-comb is safer for a beginner than deep internal texturizing. It gradually reduces bulk without committing to a single cut.

  • Work comb upward through the hair, snipping whatever stands above the comb teeth
  • Start with the comb high (removing less) — you can always lower it for a second pass
  • Check symmetry between passes

If after the first pass the sides still look bulky, take another small scissor-over-comb pass rather than cutting deeper into single sections.

If you can't see the back clearly with a hand mirror, stop and get a second mirror or a helper rather than cutting blind. Cutting what you can't see is how you end up with an uneven neckline.

Two mirrors at right angles (or a bathroom mirror + a handheld mirror) give you a usable view of the back.
