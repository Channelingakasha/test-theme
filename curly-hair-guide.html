📋 Step-by-Step Cutting Plan
1

Watch the primary video end-to-end first

Watch The One Minute Barber video (Primary tab above) without touching your hair. Then rewatch the scissor-over-comb segment and the fringe chapter immediately before you start cutting.

2

Review the curl-by-curl cutting reference

Check the Sam Villa curl-cutting card (Curl-by-Curl tab) so you understand cutting in natural curl formation and snipping at the point a curl loses its pattern.

3

Prep: style hair as normal, detangle, clip the top away

Cut dry if possible — lower-risk because you see true shrinkage and shape. (Experts disagree: Sam Villa prefers wet for control. See Tips below.)

4

Scissor-over-comb on the sides — conservative first pass

Start at the back/sides. Remove a small amount of bulk around the ears. Stop and reassess before going further — you can always take more, not less.

5

Refine ends with point-cutting where needed

Scissors vertical, tiny wedges, partial closure — reference the Joewell and Sam Villa cards (Point-Cutting tab). Go lightest on tighter 2c clumps, or keep those ends blunter.

6

Fringe / middle-part front last

Cut the fringe visibly longer than your target — curl spring will lift it up. Follow the fringe chapter (~11:00) of the primary video.

💡 Tips, Caveats & Benchmarks

Dry (recommended for beginners): Cut in natural curl formation so you see true shrinkage and shape. You know exactly what you're removing. Lower-risk for a conservative first-timer cleanup.

Wet (Sam Villa's view): "Cut the hair wet. You'll have greater control of the curl and of your sectioning with wet hair, because you'll be reading the natural silhouette." — American Salon

Both are valid. For a conservative home trim, dry is the safer starting point.

Curls spring up when dry. If you cut to your target length while wet, the hair will shrink shorter when it dries. Always leave extra — you can remove more, you can't put it back.

Applies especially to the fringe/middle-part front. Cut visibly longer, let it dry, then reassess.

Sam Villa: "If you point cut naturally curly hair, it will take the weight away from ends, causing the ends of the hair to expand and look frizzy."

On 2b (wavier) sections: point-cut fairly freely.

On 2c (tighter) clumps: keep ends blunter. Use "Weave & Blunt" — weave into the section, close your shear where you want to take weight. This removes bulk while preserving end weight that holds the curl.

Stop signal: if ends look stringy or frizzy after notching, you've taken too much end weight — stop immediately on those sections.

Scissor-over-comb is safer for a beginner than deep internal texturizing.

  • Work comb upward, snipping whatever stands above the teeth
  • Start with comb high (removing less) — lower it for a second pass if needed
  • Check symmetry between sides before each pass

If the sides still look bulky, take another small pass rather than cutting deeper into single sections.

If you can't see the back clearly with a hand mirror, stop and get a second mirror or a helper rather than cutting blind. Cutting what you can't see is how you end up with an uneven neckline.

Two mirrors at right angles (bathroom mirror + handheld) give a usable back view.
